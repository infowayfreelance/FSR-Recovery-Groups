**H2: Where Are You on the A167? Landmarks That Help Us Find You**
The A167 changes character every few miles, so "I'm on the A167" isn't enough on its own. Tell us which stretch you're on:
- **Darlington:** the town-centre roads and the section out to where the A167 crosses the A1(M) at junction 59.
- **Newton Aycliffe and Ferryhill:** the open stretch north of Darlington, including the Chilton bypass near Ferryhill.
- **Durham:** the approach roads and the section through Durham City.
- **Chester-le-Street and Birtley:** the northern stretch toward Gateshead and Newcastle.
Then add **the direction you're heading** (for example north toward Durham or south toward Darlington), **the nearest roundabout, junction, petrol station or village sign**, and **what has happened to the vehicle**. If you can, share your live location with us on WhatsApp, then call **+44 7888 502989**.

**H2: Town Streets and Open Stretches: Staying Safe on the A167**
On the busier town sections in Darlington, Durham and Chester-le-Street, switch on your hazard warning lights and, if the vehicle can still move, pull in where you are not blocking a junction, roundabout, bus stop or pedestrian crossing. Keep passengers on the pavement, away from traffic, and don't try a repair in a live lane.

On the more open stretches with higher speed limits, follow the Highway Code's advice on [breakdowns and incidents](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/the-highway-code/breakdowns-and-incidents-274-to-287): get the vehicle off the road if possible, use your hazard lights, keep your sidelights on if it's dark or visibility is poor, and wait in a place of relative safety. After a collision, call **999** first if anyone is hurt or in danger. Once everyone is safe, call us and we'll arrange recovery. For what to do next, read our guide on [what to do after a car accident in County Durham](/blog/after-a-car-accident-county-durham).

**H2: On the A167 or the A1(M)?**
The A167 runs close to the A1(M) for much of the way between Darlington and Durham, and drivers sometimes aren't sure which road they're on. If you're on the motorway, you'll see hard shoulders or emergency areas and blue signs with junction numbers; tell us the junction you last passed. Motorways have their own safety rules, so see our [A1(M) recovery page](/areas/a1m) and our guide to [breaking down on the A1(M)](/blog/broken-down-on-the-a1m). Either way, the number to call is the same: **+44 7888 502989**.
